In the age of AI and potential circular financing, I find myself asking "what do I want". This is a very hard question that I have not had to grapple with before. From a young age, I had the privilege of an extraordinarily close and supportive family structure with parents, grandparents, cousins, aunts, uncles, etc. With this supportive and caring family structure, I knew that I could trust that their advice was truly what they believed would lead to the best outcome for me. The strongest and most consistent advice across all these individual family members, I think we can all guess what it was "Go to college". So I followed their advice. I applied myself in high school, got competitive grades, played sports, was involved in STEM extracurriculars like FIRST ROBOTICS and Math league, and when I applied to college I got in. (This is not the whole story, the 2nd or maybe a tied for first signal I got from my family members was find something you love/you're passionate about). Luckily in college, my passions just so happened to align well with what I chose to major in (technically the two majors I chose, mathematics and physics. Although, I would say I am a general scientist/engineer that would have been happy and interested in nearly any science field or even science adjacent field, if that even has meaning in todays world, I will save my opinions on the work "Science" for another day). So again, I excelled in my chosen majors and I had a new set of mentors guiding me towards the "best next step". The mentors were my professors, so I'm sure we can guess what the next step was, "Get a PhD!". So what did I do? I applied to PhD programs and ended up matriculating to the University of Minnesota to do MRI research at the Center for Magnetic Resonance Research. This is all a pretty large simplification and the decisions I made were multifaceted. I had a girlfriend of 4 years at this time (who is not my wife and mother of our children), I had that awesome and extremely blessed family structure in Minnesota (which also was now extending to my wife's nearly as awesome family), I didn't have any super strong industry prospects since I'd hedged nearly all my bests during undergraduate school to advance to a PhD, etc. So now I've started a PhD, bought a house with the money I saved working during undergrad (made rent cheap living with 6 people in 4 bedroom houses, not going out and spending money at the bar, getting good grades which led to scholarships, etc.), got married, and had my first kid. Again, this was all basically just taking the advice given by others and doing it. Everyone says buy a house, and it is human nature for this to be basically self-evident (but it also minimized my monthly expenditures which leads to long term financial stability, it was more calculated than "I want a house"). My family was exemplary in instilling family values, it was only natural that I extend this by getting married (fun fact, I saved a Mall of America wristband that I got during a undergraduate welcome week activity since during this trip I was basically constantly flirting with this cute, athletic, smart, Whitty, composed girl that I had just met in the off chance that we started dating and one day she would become my wife, which she eventually did 5 years later to my overwhelming gratitude). So I had a wife and a house, next logical step in life, a kid! 9 months after we got married our first child came into this world (do the math ;) ), which was during the 3rd year of my PhD. The pregnancy was a little rocky and our first born had some medical issues for the first year and a half of life that scared mom and dad, but he has grown into the most wonderful, beautiful, healthy and active kid that I could possibly even hope for. OK, back on track to where I'm at at this point. I'm 24, have a house wife and child, and am pursuing a PhD. I am really enjoying the intellectual stimulation of all the projects I am working on for my PhD and honestly having a blast, but after I get my first paper accepted, I start to get this strange feeling. I know that this MRI coil that I built for the paper has great, arguably state of the art performance, but I know it is never really going to be useful to anyone else besides our lab. There is almost no chance that an MRI vender is going to manufacture this coil. It is too hard to build and therefore to expensive to manufacture. So all of this work and effort I put into this coil building project is going to have a limited direct impact. The lessons I learned, friends I made, knowledge I published, laughs I had, frustration I felt, etc., are all impacts that this coil project had that are intangible but need to be mentioned and accounted for. But as for generating value for other people and being used by a larger audience, not so much. On the personal side, my wife and I had been talking about having a second child for a while and after careful planning we came up with an ideal second child birth month and year and worked backward 9 months from there and started trying. Again, success and we officially had a second baby countdown clock running. While we were pretty comfortable on my PhD salary and my wife's engineering salary (oh yeah, she is 100% a baddie, I could write multiple blogs dedicated solely to her), childcare for 2 children was not going to be feasible. So I needed to graduate from my PhD and either get a higher academic job or switch to industry. I have left it out of this story so far, but one of my other deep passions is teaching. I was a tutor for the SMART learning commons for 3 years during my undergraduate days and consistently was either the top or 2nd best rated peer tutor in the SMART learning commons. During my PhD, I was a TA for 2 semesters and the students from my first TA course gave me probably my most cherished memory from my PhD by giving me the honor of best TA award for the UMN BME graduating class of 2023. I cannot express how much this award meant to me and still means to me, it nearly brings me tears of joy whenever I think about it... 

Anyway, so I did yearn to stay in academia but was a little disillusioned with the valley of death phenomena, where you get a prototype working that could be the best in the world, but it is just not economically feasible/ commercially viable, and therefore the idea/device never impacts the broader world and just stays in an academic lab. Furthermore, I did not like the general opinion of academia where you need to move around anywhere you can possibly find a position and staying in the same place where you got your PhD is frowned upon. It even has its own derogatory word "academic inbreeding". This notion of force movement just does not play well with my family values nor the roots I had spent years putting down. So I decided to look for industry jobs to learn how actual commercial products are made, to have a broader market impact, to force my PI to let me graduate (sorry Greg!), to enable me to maintain my tight family connections and proximity, (to get a little bump in pay, you know it is a factor so it deserves mention, but it wasn't THAT large of a factor). After a few months of looking, a nearly perfectly matched job came up "MRI physics, modeling and simulation principle engineer". Wow, reading this description it was nearly verbatim what I was looking for and what I felt I was qualified for (honestly, this along with all the other blessings I've had must have been on of the gifts that I've received from God). The only problem was that it was a slightly higher title than what is typical for new PhD grads switching to industry. But what the heck, it cannot hurt to just apply! After a few rounds of interviews and months waiting on hold, I got the position! I now understand that this is actually a pretty common cooperate thing, waiting till next quarter or fiscal year before adding on personnel costs haha. 

At this same time that I started, LLM based AI had just started to explode. The hype was real. The market valuations were real. NVIDIA had increase like 1,000% in a single year. Same with Micon, Qualcomm, Broadcom, Amazon, google, SK Hynex, Intel was finally back to a good stock, AMD lagged a little but still. It is a crazy time while I'm currently writing this introspection on 2026-08-01. But this crazy time and stock valuations has lead me to contemplate something that I have not really had to wrestle with before. Why? What is the purpose? What do I want to do next and what should I do next? There is a real fear in some people that we are actively creating an overlord class that is going to own everything. Just look at the stock evaluations. If these people just decided to sell and buy land and houses, they could own everything. To hedge against this, it makes me want to buy land and real assets. However, I then think about what the point of "owning" the land/assets really is? Ultimately, I do not think that I want to be a farmer. I would get too bored I believe. I do have a few things that I would like to try that require land, but I do not have the time to dedicate to these ventures right now with my current job that I enjoy and that provides WELL for my family that also helps produce devices that help real-world patients. So if I bought land, it would just end up being a burden to me and would cost a lot of money which I could be spending on other things that would bring me more joy/satisfaction (Paying taxes and interest to other people just to be able to call a piece of dirt that I do not even see "mine" on paper, that is a silly idea as it currently stands). Then I think that this same logic applies to the people that make the newest/ next-gen LLMs. They could sell out and purchase a bunch of land, but then they become beholden to the land. The land is now their owners and them the slave for having "purchased" the land. For this property that they "own" will be ripped away from them should they stop paying for the privilege of calling it their own with taxes. In this way, the land needs to be put to use, or else it will be given up/ taken by the society that created these property tax laws.  Good idea, bad idea, I am really split. I find that on first glance it is a hedge against dynasties/ inheritance which I could agree with. However, if these people have enough means and want to manage I could see that "renting" out the land for others to work would enable the land owners to not have to work indefinitely, instead they have others work the field for them while they claim ownership of the land in perpetuity. 

Anyway, owning land is not feasible nor even a real want for me currently, although I love to garden and plant trees to bear fruit and have done the most I can to this aim at my current house (14 fruit trees, multiple berry bushed, etc.). OK, so what is the issue I take with maintaining my current position at my current job? Nothing really. It is just not quite as impactful as I'd like. I'm helping to create class III medical devices. These are basically devices for people with no other option. While there is a romantic notion of helping the most vulnerable with these pursuits, I still feel like it is not as wide reaching as I'd want in an ideal world. What kind of reach are you looking for you might ask now? Good question, I'm not entirely sure... I do not seek to help create cell phones. I do not see this as an ultimate good that I am doing for the people that are going to use the product. Many consumer products fall into this same category for me. Yes they can be used by the masses, but it does not mean that they are good and should exist. I think what I really yearn for is to develop a system of resilience for my community/country. That is a broad and vague claim for a want. What do I mean by this? I think we can start by describing a good example of what I'm shooting for. Smarter Every Day's video on his grill scrubber that he wanted to make in America [video pt 1](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=3ZTGwcHQfLY)  [video pt 2](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=vS6HEes8daw)! Create something that lets other people not only consume your product, but have the thing that you're creating, the development, manufacturing, distribution, go towards creating something of value and positive outcomes for everyone involved. This video series exemplifies this too the letter. The manufacturing that they learned kept this craft alive in his local area. This skilled knowledge didn't just die with the retiree. The product is going to last so it's not going to end up getting recalled and thrown away in land fills which pollutes and wastes. He had integrity, doing something hard because he made a moral judgement that this increase in cost and effort would be better to exist in the world as compared to just having it be made by others oversees. It connects the people who built it to the people that are using this. That connection and cohesiveness has a value. It's hard to measure by I believe it to be extremely high. They are setting an example for others by just physically showing "this can be done if you will it so". That is what I mean by a resilient system. 4) Reduction on everyone's dependance on imports
5) The re-introduction of leisure time

7) Building back the trades
8) Teaching the next generation to be curious and original while also teaching them real skills
